IT Portfolio & Governance on Elementum

We see IT portfolio and governance as eight accountable process areas that one technology ask travels through: it arrives as demand, earns a funding decision, is tested against capacity, gets delivered, and is finally measured for the value it promised — with dependencies and vendors managed alongside, and a governance foundation setting the rules for all of it.

Each of those areas becomes an Elementum App with its own record and lifecycle. This document sets out those eight apps, the supporting records that hang off them, the shared configuration they read, the outside systems they consume, and the automations that move work between them — so each piece can be reviewed before authoring starts.

1 · How the pieces fit

Four kinds of building block, chosen by behavior

The eight apps are the process boundaries people work inside. Everything else is chosen by how it behaves, not by convenience: work with a lifecycle is an App, lightweight repeating work owned by a record is a supporting task entity, reusable configuration is an Element, facts owned elsewhere are Tables, and movement between them is Automation.

8 Apps

Process areas

Each area owns one primary record and its lifecycle: Demand Request, Investment Decision, Capacity Plan, Initiative, Outcome Record, Dependency and Vendor Engagement, plus the governance model itself.

75 records

Supporting tasks

The repeating detail that hangs off a primary record: Cost Line, Milestone, Decision Condition, Assessment, Status Report and similar items — many rows, no lifecycle of their own.

Elements

Configuration

Products, objectives, demand types, gates, scores, authority thresholds, teams and other reference data that changes behavior without a redeploy.

Tables

Systems of record

Work items, actual cost, workforce, telemetry, contracts, invoices and vendor risk—read from the systems that already own them.

Automations are the arrows. They validate, route, create handoff records, start approvals, enforce gates, lock baselines, detect breaches and send notifications. They are not another record type.

4 · Supporting records

The repeating detail that hangs off a record

A business case has many cost lines; an initiative has many milestones, risks and status reports; an approval carries decision conditions. These repeat, are owned by a parent record, and need their own rows — but they do not run a lifecycle of their own. Elementum has a dedicated task entity for exactly this shape.

What a task entity gives us

Fields, records, status options, automations, layouts and relationships — the right fit for action items, cost lines, milestones, review items and other owned supporting work.

Where the line sits

Task entities have no stages and do not host AI agents. Anything that genuinely moves through governed stages stays an App, and AI capability lives on an App.

Why the distinction matters

Getting this right is what keeps the eight apps clean. Modelling cost lines as app stages would bloat the lifecycle; modelling a real governed process as a task would strip the gates it needs.

Not every supporting item needs its own entity. Cost Line, Milestone and Decision Condition clearly repeat and need their own rows. Others do not: a Composite Score is likely a calculated field, Status History is likely native record behavior, and a Governance Reporting Pack is generated output rather than stored work. Process and product owners should walk the list app by app.

6 · Client inputs

What we need from the client, by application

The solution structure is reusable; the operating model is not ours to invent. Each process area needs named owners, policy decisions and source data before it can meet the acceptance criteria.

Start data readiness early. The hard part is usually attribution: Portfolio ID on work items and finance records, product or initiative tags on cloud spend, outcome keys in telemetry, and stable vendor/contract references.
