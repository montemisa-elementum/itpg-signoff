IT Portfolio & Governance on Elementum

We see IT portfolio and governance as two lifecycles: the demand arriving from the business, and the investments the organization decides to fund. The whole solution is moving records through those two lifecycles honestly — and everything else exists to make that possible.

This document sets out the Elementum components that implement it — the two apps that hold the process, the records that configure it, the work that hangs off it, the automations that move it, and the systems it reads — so each piece can be approved before any authoring starts.

1 · The two buckets

Two apps hold the process

Demand Request and Portfolio Item each have stages, and ITPG is the business of moving a record from one stage to the next with a recorded reason. A request enters as demand; if it needs funding or governance it becomes a portfolio item, and the same identifier follows it to the end.

App

Demand Request

handle DMD · namespace demand-request · wave 1

Every technology ask lands here — business requests, technical debt, and security remediation alike. Its job is to end with a recorded decision, not to become a project.

  1. Capture — one form that changes by demand type; drafts can be saved and resumed.
  2. Validate — submit enforces the mandatory set, so triage does not start on half a request.
  3. Route — the affected product determines the accountable owner automatically.
  4. Screen — check open demand, in-flight items, and the engineering backlog for overlap.
  5. Dispose — route to backlog, accept as investment, merge, defer, reject, or redirect, with a reason.

Most demand ends here, on an existing product team's backlog. That is the intended outcome, not a failure.

App

Portfolio Item

handle PFI · namespace portfolio-item · waves 2–3

The funded bet. One record holds the business case, the decision, the baseline, and delivery health — so nothing is retyped into a second system after approval.

  1. Qualify — structured case: options, cost by year, benefit owner, measurable outcomes.
  2. Score — the published scoring model ranks it against everything else, including mandatory work.
  3. Decide — value and type select the approval authority; conditions are recorded, not remembered.
  4. Baseline — approved funding is locked, which is what makes later variance meaningful.
  5. Execute — cadence, health with mandatory commentary, milestones, and control gates with evidence.
  6. Realize — outcomes stay open after delivery closes until the value is measured.

2 · The supporting cast

Everything else does one of four jobs

The useful split is not “app or not an app” — it is what a component does for the two lifecycles. Every piece in this build falls into one of these four jobs, which is also how the map and the explorer filters are organized.

1 Elements

Prerequisite data — what the stages need to know

Who is allowed to approve at what value, which categories exist, which scoring weights and gate model apply, which products and owners are real. These answer who, what kind, which model, which authority.

They are elements rather than apps because they have no lifecycle of their own: a governance admin edits records, and the behavior of both lifecycles changes without a redeploy.

2 Child apps

Structured work that hangs off a record

Cost lines, clarification requests, conditions of approval, milestones, gate records, risks and issues. Each exists because one field cannot hold “many rows, each with its own owner and dates”.

Some gate the lifecycle — open conditions block Execute, a gate needs evidence or a waiver. Others simply feed it, the way cost lines roll up into total cost of ownership.

3 Automations

The moves themselves

Submit, route to the accountable owner, mint the Portfolio ID, match the approval authority, lock the baseline, flag an overdue status. These are the transitions — the part that must be exact and identical every time.

Not apps, and not agents: an agent can summarize or challenge, but nothing an agent does changes a stage. The register is in Rules & access.

4 Table ports

Facts from outside

The engineering backlog, finance actuals, cloud spend, the staff directory. Read-only feeds with no stages of their own, whose entire purpose is that nobody retypes what another system already knows.

These are the components that depend on you: each needs a join key before the data can be attributed. See what you configure.

What deliberately stays a field

A disposition is a status plus a reason. Health rating, narrative, and recovery action are fields. Status history is native to the platform. The source data inventory listed several of these as separate tables; most should not become apps.

What is deferred

Capacity, outcomes, dependencies, vendors, and scenario planning are grouped into optional modules at the end of this document. They add more of the same four jobs — each is a decision on its own, and several wait on an integration.

6 · What you configure

What we need from you, and when

The apps, fields, rules, and automations are the product. Your structure, your approvers, your thresholds, and your data are configuration — which is what makes the same build reusable, and what we need from you to stand it up.

The long pole is usually the join key, not the connection. A feed is only useful once your work items carry the Portfolio ID, your cost records carry it too, and your cloud spend is tagged. That is work on your side, and it is worth starting before the integration wave.

7 · Optional modules

Grouped separately, decided separately

These are ordinary apps, elements, and table ports — nothing special about how they are built. They are set apart because the core build works without them, each is its own go or no-go, and several wait on an integration that does not exist yet. Each block says where it would plug into the flows in section 4.

Skipping a module costs you that capability and nothing else: no field on Demand Request or Portfolio Item depends on one, and a module can be added in a later wave without rework. Do not write acceptance criteria against a module until it has been selected.
