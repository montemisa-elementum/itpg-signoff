Pain evidence

The operating problems behind the requirements

These are not generic ITPG assumptions; they come from the supplied Pain Points sheet.

Manual governance

Gate approval is closing a task, milestones round-trip through Excel, KPI/benefit data is typed manually, and the executive pack takes almost a week.

Weak traceability

Owners are stale, role names vary, estimates do not reconcile to cost, RAG is self-reported and business-case data is retyped after approval.

Fragmented systems

Work spans seven-plus tools, teams choose their own method, exception programs sit outside the system, and there is no dependency tracker.

Acceptance

“Done” is defined per application

The Acceptance Criteria repeats the same six evidence dimensions for all eight applications. Each application is expected to stand up its own evidence, which is why each one is designed as its own process area.

Configure

Forms, models, thresholds, workflows, views and client data are configured and reconciled.

Test & UAT

Must Haves pass and an end-to-end scenario runs on representative enterprise data.

Train

Named role holders can operate the application and admins can configure it unaided.

Document

Process, configuration, data model, field and reporting definitions are agreed.

Integration

Each application’s source-system and cross-application handoffs reconcile.

Sign-off

The accountable process owner accepts the application and its support model.

Missing input

The process document still matters

Requirements describe capabilities and acceptance. They do not completely define actors, stage transitions, exception paths, record ownership or the exact handoff between the eight areas.
